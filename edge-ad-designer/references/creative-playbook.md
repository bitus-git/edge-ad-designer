# Creative playbook: what makes edgy ads perform

**Evidence base, honestly stated.** The largest study of political ad experiments (Hewitt et al., APSR 2024: 146 experiments, 617 ads, more than 500k respondents) found that common theories of "what persuades" have limited and context-dependent predictive power. Single-ad persuasion effects are small (Coppock, Hill & Vavreck, Sci. Adv. 2020). **Treat everything below as a way to generate hypotheses, then test many distinct concepts.**

## 1. Hooks

- **Statics have one frame.** The headline *is* the hook: 3–7 words, the largest element, readable in under a second at thumbnail size.
- **Video:** show the claim or payoff by second 3. TikTok reports that 63%+ of its top-CTR videos show the key message in the first 3 seconds. Burn in captions (most viewing is with sound off). The first frame must work as a static.
- **Google ABCD for YouTube:** Attention, Branding (early but small), Connection (emotion), Direction (a clear CTA).
- **One idea per ad.** A second idea becomes a second ad.
- **The hook must carry tension** (surprise, injustice, absurdity), not a topic label.

| Archetype | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | "Everything you heard about X is wrong" | "Vaping isn't the problem. Banning it is." |
| Specific stat | A big number + the source on the image | "64% went back to cigarettes." *Source, year* |
| Implicating question | Points at the decision-maker, not the viewer's attributes | "Who decided adults can't buy menthol?" |
| Us vs. them | People vs. an institution or decision | "They banned your flavour. Then raised your tax." |
| Satire | A mock-official notice from an obviously fictional agency | "Department of Your Business: Form 27-B. Permission to Make Adult Choices: DENIED" |
| Myth / fact | Strikethrough + correction | "~~Vapes are as harmful as cigarettes~~" |
| Concrete loss | A real price, a real thing lost | "Your $8 pack. Now $13. Thanks, [Council]." |
| Real-news frame | A real, dated headline + your take | Screenshot → "Here's what they didn't say." |
| Deadline | A real date + a single action | "Vote is Thursday. Your rep is undecided." |

## 2. Format menu (each is a distinct "concept slot")

Meta's Andromeda-era delivery treats creative as the targeting, and near-duplicates collapse into one ad. Diversity has to be **conceptual** (angle, frame, format, face, setting), not recoloured headlines. *[verify]* the agency claims about specific similarity thresholds.

1. **Tweet or X-post style.** Use the org's real account. Good for contrarian takes.
2. **Notes-app or iMessage-style dramatisation.** First person and confessional ("Things I can't buy in my state anymore:"). Keep it stylised, with no real OS chrome.
3. **Meme structure.** Two buttons, starter pack, Drake-style. Use original art, not copyrighted photos.
4. **Stat card.** A huge number, a one-line meaning, a source. This is your credibility anchor.
5. **This vs. that.** Split screen: "Cigarettes: legal everywhere. Safer alternative: banned."
6. **Mock-official document.** A permit, warning label or rejected form, using Horatian (gentle) satire.
7. **Carousel.** Card 1 is the hook, cards 2–4 are proof, the last card is the CTA. Ideal for myth-busting.
8. **Bold editorial poster.** Huge type, high contrast, one provocative line.
9. **Talking head (9:16).** A real adult consumer, shop owner or former smoker speaking to camera. Faces and testimony do much of the persuading.
10. **Headline card.** A news-energy statement in your *own* brand system (see format-traffic-lights #1).

Lo-fi and "ugly" ads: Motion's 2025 data (500+ DTC brands) found 42% of top ads were deliberately lo-fi, versus 14% of all ads. That data is mostly video, and the effect for lo-fi statics is unproven. The principle: **native beats polished when it reads as content**. Because lo-fi is now common, mix it with bold graphic work for contrast.

## 3. Persuasion and framing

- **Moral reframing** (Feinberg & Willer): argue in the *audience's* values, not yours. Build variants by moral foundation:
  - **Liberty**: autonomy, adult choice, government overreach. Speaks to libertarians and the right.
  - **Care/harm**: harm reduction saves lives. Speaks to the left, public health and the centre.
  - **Fairness**: regressive taxes; enforcement that falls unequally. Speaks to the left and working class.
  - **Anti-cronyism**: who benefits from the ban? Works across the spectrum.
  - **Absurdity/satire**: for reach and shares.
- **Loss vs. gain:** make losses concrete, personal and immediate. Evidence across contexts is mixed, so test both.
- **Identity:** "People like us…" mobilises. Turn users into a constituency.
- **Villain:** out-group language strongly predicts shares (Rathje et al., PNAS 2021), but engagement is not persuasion.
  - Aim villain framing at **institutions, policies and official acts**, never at groups of people.
  - Attacks energise the base and can repel persuadables.
- **Humour vs. outrage:** satire spreads but persuades weakly. Gentle satire beats harsh mockery. Use humour for reach and persuadables, and outrage for mobilising the base.
- **Credibility:** every stat gets a visible short source on the image plus the full citation in the caption or landing page. Use real prices, bill numbers, dates and people.
- **Lean into breadth.** With broad targeting, the frame decides who sees the ad: a liberty-coded ad finds libertarians, a price ad finds working-class voters.

## 4. Static design craft

- **Contrast stops the scroll.** Use saturated yellow, red or orange against the white and grey feed UI, or white on near-black. Text must pass WCAG contrast.
- **Hierarchy has three levels at most:**
  - the hook, in a heavy condensed sans
  - a support line of 8–15 words
  - source, logo, CTA and disclaimer (at least ~24px on a 1080 canvas)
- **Two typefaces and three colours at most**, plus black and white.
- **One focal point.** Squint test: blur the image and the hook should still read.
- **Faces:** real-looking adults looking at the camera or toward the headline. Never a real private person without consent, and never a fabricated photo of a public figure.
- **Brand early but small** (a corner logo). The "Paid for by" must never be contradicted by the creative.

### Specs and safe zones (confirm with each platform's templates or preview)

Deliver 4:5, 9:16 and 1:1 for every concept, **re-composed** for each size rather than cropped.

## 5. Competitive research

- **Meta Ad Library** (facebook.com/ads/library): filter by "Issues, elections or politics". Issue ads stay visible for 7 years with spend, impressions, demographics and the payer.
  - Search opponents and allies, sort by impressions, and treat long-running, high-spend ads as likely winners.
  - Catalogue each ad's hook, format, frame and CTA.
- Also check the **Google Ads Transparency Center** and **TikTok Creative Center** (commercial format trends).

## 6. Testing

**Test order:**
1. concept and frame
2. format
3. hook (3–5 variants within the winning concept)
4. visual execution (face vs. no face, colour, illustration vs. photo)
5. CTA and landing page

**Process:**
- Run 3–6 conceptually distinct ads per ad set (practitioners often run more).
- Judge after enough spend, roughly 3–5 days *[heuristic]*.
- **Metrics by stage:**
  - thumb-stop (3-second views ÷ impressions) and hold rate
  - link CTR
  - cost per action (signature, email sent)
  - for persuasion goals, Brand Lift or survey tests, since clicks don't measure attitude change
- Iterate on winners: keep the concept, generate new hooks and formats. Kill losers fast. Refresh before frequency fatigue sets in.
- **Tag every ad** (frame / format / hook type / villain / CTA) so patterns show up across tests. Intuition won't find them.
