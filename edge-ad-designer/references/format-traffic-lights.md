# Format traffic lights: the gimmick, why it's banned, and the edge-safe version

Use this whenever someone asks for a "banned" or "too good" format. The rule of thumb:

- **Quality penalty** (🟡) means borrow the psychology.
- **Deception** (🔴) means rebuild it honestly.
- **Legal and allowed** (🟢) means push it all the way.

Researched 2026-10-27.

| # | Format | Light | Why platforms restrict it | Edge-safe version (use this) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fake "BREAKING" or news chyron, imitation outlet branding, fake headline** | 🔴 | Deception and impersonation. It is the classic scam creative, and Google treats false association as egregious. Legal exposure: Lanham Act (trade dress), FTC native-ad and Impersonation Rule, UK CAP 2.1, EU UCPD Annex I(11). | **News energy in your own brand.** Use "NEW DATA:", "JUST PASSED:", "TODAY:" or "VOTE THURSDAY:" with a real date and your logo, in a bold headline-card style that isn't a TV chyron. Or use a **real** headline screenshot, unaltered and dated, with the outlet named, framed by your brand and commentary ("As reported by…"). Label advertorials "Sponsored by [Org]" at the top. |
| 2 | **Fake UI**: play buttons on statics, close buttons, notification badges, OS alerts, fake DMs, copies of platform UI | 🔴 | Deception and accidental clicks. Banned by name on Google, TikTok (Apr 2026) and Snap (its own UI), and enforced on Meta. The inflated CTR doesn't come with real intent. | **Real video** with a motion hook instead of a fake play button. A notification-*inspired* illustration (stylised, no real OS chrome, no red badge count). A text-thread *dramatisation* that is clearly a story, with the brand visible and no iOS or Android system styling. |
| 3 | **Gore and shock imagery** | 🔴/🟡 | User experience, brand safety and protecting vulnerable users. Meta bans exposed organs even in health contexts. Google bans using negative life events to pressure people. | **Consequence through data and human stakes.** A stark single number, a real person's testimony, a closed shop front, an empty shelf. Loss framing is fine. Gore and fake urgency are not. |
| 4 | **Clickbait or withheld information** ("You won't believe…") | 🟡 (✗ on Google and TikTok) | Quality. Meta lowers delivery and can downgrade the whole Page or domain. Google bans "click to find out" and secret-reveal framing. | **Make the headline the claim**: "Flavour bans pushed X% back to cigarettes (Source, 2025)". If you want curiosity, open the loop *and close it on the image*: "The number regulators left out: 64%." |
| 5 | **Engagement bait** ("Like if you agree", "Tag a friend", "Comment YES") | 🟡 | The clearest case of a format being punished because it games metrics. Meta demotes it. | **A purposeful ask**: "Tell us your state and we'll send your rep's contact." Native polls (IG Story or X poll). A question that invites a real answer. |
| 6 | **Personal attributes** ("Are you a smoker?", "You vape, so…") | 🔴 on Meta and Snap | Privacy and discrimination law (health status, etc.), not performance. | **Address the topic, not the viewer**: "Smokers deserve better options." "1 million adults in [state] who vape could lose access." "For adults who switched: know your rights." Third person or group nouns. |
| 7 | **Before/after** | 🟡/🔴 for bodies | Exaggerated claims and body-image harm. | Before/after **policy** charts are fine: "Before the ban / after the ban" with real sourced data. Never show bodies or health transformations. |
| 8 | **Real public figures**: unauthorised likeness, implied endorsement, deepfakes, doctored photos | 🔴 | Fraud (celebrity-bait scams), election integrity, right of publicity, FEC fraudulent misrepresentation, state deepfake laws. No platform has a clear satire exception for paid ads. | **Quote the public record.** A card reading 'On [date], [Official] said: "…"' with the source, plus a press photo you have the rights to. Criticise official acts and policies. For satire, use illustration or caricature that is clearly not a photo, a clearly fictional agency name, and AI disclosure where required. Never imply an endorsement. |
| 9 | **Text-heavy image** | 🟢 | Meta dropped the 20% rule in 2020. A soft delivery effect may remain *[verify]*. | Text-forward is fine for advocacy. Keep one dominant claim and one support line on the image, and put the argument in the caption. |
| 10 | **ALL CAPS, "!!!", emoji spam** | 🟡 (✗ on Google and LinkedIn) | Editorial and quality rules. | All caps on one or two words at most, set in a heavy condensed typeface that looks loud without breaking editorial rules. Normal punctuation. |
| 11 | **Fake countdown or false deadline** | 🔴 | Deception (FTC dark patterns, Google false urgency). | **Real deadlines only**: "Comment period closes Nov 14." "Hearing Thursday 10am." Real dates hit harder anyway. |
| 12 | **Native, UGC, memes, tweet or notes-app style** | 🟢 | Allowed. It becomes a problem only if it impersonates a real user, account or outlet, or hides the sponsor. | Use your **real** org account in tweet-style posts, or a clearly generic placeholder. Recreate meme *structures* with original art; don't use copyrighted meme photos. Creator posts need the paid-partnership label, and on Meta political creator content needs the branded-content plus SIEP setup. |
| 13 | **Attack and comparative ads** naming officials, regulators or companies | 🟢 on Meta, Google, X and Snap with SIEP setup | Defamation and accuracy rules. Snap: nothing about someone's personal life. TikTok and LinkedIn ban it outright. | **Name the decision and the record, not the character**: "Commissioner X's ban would cost Y jobs (Source)." That beats "X is lying to you." |
| 14 | **AI-generated imagery** | 🟡 | Disclosure is required for photorealistic synthetic or altered people and events in issue ads (Meta, Google), and Meta auto-labels since Jun 2026. | Use AI for clearly illustrative or stylised visuals. Disclose anything photorealistic. Never make a real person appear to say or do something they didn't. |

## The arbitrage that *is* legitimate

- **Category arbitrage.** Your opponents may not bother getting SIEP authorisation, Google election verification or X certification. If you do the paperwork, you get the inventory they can't use.
- **Platform arbitrage.** Policy advocacy is closed on TikTok and LinkedIn but open on Meta, Google, X and Snap (outside the EU). Put budget where it can run. On closed platforms, use organic posts and properly disclosed creator content where the platform's organic rules allow it.
- **Format arbitrage.** Whatever is penalised for quality on Meta (e.g. text-dense statics) may perform well on X or Reddit, where the native feed is text-heavy. Still check each platform's editorial rules.
- **Search arbitrage (Google).** A pure issue ad that names no officeholder is an ordinary ad in the US, with ordinary targeting. Naming a senator makes it an election ad with restricted targeting. Decide deliberately whether naming the person is worth it.
